Solar Eclipses

A journey through some of the most spectacular eclipses of the last few years — and the total eclipse coming on August 12, 2026.

02 / 06
🌑

April 8, 2024

The Great North American Total Eclipse

The Moon completely covered the Sun along a path through Mexico, the United States and Canada.

During totality, the Sun's corona became visible and daytime skies became dramatically darker.

03 / 06
⭕

October 2, 2024

The Ring of Fire

An annular eclipse crossed parts of the Pacific and southern South America.

Because the Moon appeared slightly smaller than the Sun, a bright ring remained around it.

04 / 06
🌘

March 29, 2025

A Partial Eclipse

Parts of Europe and other regions saw the Moon cover only part of the Sun.

Partial phases are especially important for eclipse safety: proper solar viewing protection is required.

05 / 06
⭕

February 17, 2026

Another Annular Eclipse

An annular eclipse crossed parts of Antarctica and the far south of South America.

It was another chance to see the Moon line up with the Sun while leaving a glowing ring.

The Eclipse Phases

1🌘 First ContactMoon first touches the Sun.
2🌒 Partial EclipseMore of the Sun becomes covered.
3🌑 Totality BeginsThe Sun's bright face disappears.
4🌑 TotalityThe corona can become visible.
5🌘 Totality EndsBright sunlight returns.
6🌒 Final ContactThe eclipse is completely over.

Why Doesn't It Happen Every Month?

The Moon orbits Earth on a tilted path.

🌞 Sun
🌍 Earth
🌙 Moon

The three bodies need to line up closely enough for the Moon's shadow to reach Earth. Most months, the Moon passes slightly above or below the Sun from our viewpoint.
